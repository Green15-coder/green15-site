Does the filter discriminate?

Established

Every candidate the engine rejected was graded too. That makes the only comparison that matters possible: what it took, against what it passed on. Bars show the 95% confidence interval; the vertical mark is the observed rate.

+10.4 points, z = +3.19. The intervals don't overlap. Passes land on a coin flip; selections don't. This is the first sample in the project able to show that, because it is the first one where rejected candidates were also graded.

It does not say the book made money. At these prices it returned +1.70%.

Where the edge is spent

269 priced bets

A filter that picks ten points above chance and returns almost nothing is not broken — its output is being handed back at the window. Each band is measured against the break-even its own prices demand.

One band is the whole leak. −199 to −151 is 28% of bet volume and −7.65 units. A process clearing ~59% cannot pay a 63.2% break-even. The engine now hard-rejects it, while exempting ≤ −200, which is profitable and a different population — heavy favorites the model also rates highly, not mid-priced coin flips.

That rule was derived from these same 267 rows. The honest test is the next hundred bets, which is why rejected candidates keep getting graded.

What this does not establish

Read before using
  • Profitability. +1.70% on 267 bets is inside the noise. Discrimination and profit are separate claims and only the first has support.
  • Closing-line value. Negative across every bucket, including passes — the signature of a measurement fault, not a skill verdict. Prices are read at one book and closes at another, so it is largely reporting the spread between them.
  • Clover leaf scoring. Historical rows carry no leaves, so their Clover tier is meaningless. Tiers here are the engine's own PLAY/LEAN/FADE buckets, kept separate on purpose.
  • Anything at n < 20. FADE is 6-for-6. That is a coin landing heads six times.
