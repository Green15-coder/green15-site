Does the filter discriminate?

Every candidate the engine rejected was graded too, so what it took can be compared against what it passed on. Bars are 95% confidence intervals; the mark is the observed rate.

Are the probabilities honest?

When the engine says 60%, does it come in about six times in ten? Every edge, every EV and every stake is computed from these numbers, so this is the test that decides whether any of them mean anything. Pushes are excluded — a refunded bet has no outcome to compare a probability against.

Does the ranking predict?

Win rate by the Clover tier a pick carried when it was made. Until the score was written onto the ledger this could not be asked at all — every graded row was scored after the fact, with no evidence attached, so the whole history sat in one bucket at 49.8%. Rows with no stamped tier are counted separately, not folded into Weak: putting them there would fill the bottom bucket with coin flips and make the ranking look calibrated because its worst grade matched chance.

What is each leaf worth?

The Clover score gives all fifteen leaves one point each, which assumes they are equally informative. Nothing has ever tested that. Lift is the only number that settles it: how much more often a pick won when the leaf was green than when it was red. A leaf with no lift is spending a point of the scale and giving nothing back; a leaf with negative lift is pushing the ranking the wrong way and is worse than absent. Coverage matters as much — a leaf dark on most of the board cannot move a ranking whatever its lift, and neither can one that is green on almost everything.

Does the model beat the market?

The engine shrinks every projection 30% of the way from the de-vigged line toward its own number, then caps it four points out. The obvious complaint is that the cap is what flattens the calibration curve — so this tests it. Brier score for the market alone, the raw projection, and the anchored blend, against the same outcomes. Lower is better. The bar to clear is the market, not the base rate: a projection that beats always-guessing but loses to the posted line has not earned the right to move a price, because quoting the line was free. The weight sweep shows where the optimum actually sits.
